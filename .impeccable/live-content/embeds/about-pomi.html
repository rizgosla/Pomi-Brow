The difference

Permanent makeup is not the same from one artist to another

Two artists may offer the same service names, but the experience and result may feel completely different. Permanent makeup depends on mapping, pressure, pigment choice, skin reading, comfort, sanitation, and the artist’s eye for balance.

A good artist does not copy the same look onto every client. The shape, color, softness, density, and placement should match your face and how you want to look after healing.

Artist style

Different artists bring different artistry to permanent makeup

Permanent makeup has technique, but it also has style. The artist’s taste, patience, and design eye shape the final result.

The natural artist

A natural-style artist focuses on soft edges, gentle color, and results that look good without heavy makeup. This approach is best for clients who want to look fresh, not overdone.

The bold artist

A bold-style artist may create more dramatic brows, stronger eyeliner, or more noticeable color. This may fit some clients, but it may not fit someone who wants a softer healed result.

The technical artist

A technical artist focuses on symmetry, pigment placement, needle control, and how the design heals. This matters because fresh work and healed work do not look the same.

The client-focused artist

A client-focused artist listens first. Pomi loves learning what each client wants, then designing a result that fits the person, not a trend.

Skin knowledge

Skin type changes the technique

Permanent makeup is not only about drawing a nice shape. The skin decides how pigment holds, how soft the result becomes, and how the healed color looks.

Pomi adjusts her approach based on skin tone, oily or non-oily skin, sensitive skin, mature skin, texture, prior work, and the condition of the treatment area. This helps the service look better after healing.

Oily skin

Oily skin may soften fine strokes faster. Powder brows or shading may be a better fit for some clients because the healed result often needs more structure.

Dry skin

Dry skin may hold detail well, but aftercare still matters. Overworking dry or delicate skin is not the goal.

Mature skin

Mature skin often needs a gentle design with softer edges. A harsh shape may not heal as naturally.

Skin tone and undertone

Pigment choice should be based on the client’s natural coloring and the healed result, not only the color in the bottle.

FAQ

Choosing a Permanent Makeup Artist FAQ

Why does the permanent makeup artist matter?

The artist affects the design, pigment choice, technique, comfort, sanitation, and healed result. Permanent makeup should be customized to the client, not copied from a template.

What should I look for in a permanent makeup artist?

Look for experience, healed results, client reviews, clean practices, clear communication, and an artist who understands different skin types and tones.

Why is skin type important for permanent makeup?

Skin type affects how pigment holds and heals. Oily, dry, mature, sensitive, textured, or previously tattooed skin may need different technique or service recommendations.

Why are materials important in permanent makeup?

Pigment, needles, single-use tools, barriers, and aftercare products all affect the appointment experience and healed result.

Yes. Pomi designs around the client’s natural features, skin, lifestyle, and comfort level so the final result feels personal.
