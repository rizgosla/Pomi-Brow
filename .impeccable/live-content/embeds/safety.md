Permanent makeup involves the skin, so safety starts with the full appointment process. A safer appointment includes a clean studio, fresh disposable barriers, single-use tools, careful pigment handling, gloves, masks, and clear aftercare instructions.

I want clients to feel comfortable asking questions. You should know how the space is prepared, what tools are used, and what to do before and after your appointment.

_Single-use tools_

## Why single-use permanent makeup tools matter

Single-use tools help reduce cross-contamination risk because they are used for one client and then safely discarded.

<!-- pomi-wide-card -->

### Needles and cartridges

Needles and cartridges should be new for the appointment. I open these items in front of you so you know they are fresh and have not been used on anyone else.

### Pigment containers

Pigment containers are used for one client only. This helps keep the pigment handling clean and prevents shared-use contamination.

### Disposable supplies

Disposable tools and supplies are used during the appointment and discarded after the session. This keeps the process clean from client to client.

### No reuse of single-use items

Items designed for single use should never be cleaned and reused. They should be disposed of after the appointment.

_Who should ask first_

## Who should ask before booking permanent makeup?

Some clients may still be good candidates, but timing matters. Please ask before booking if any of these apply to you.

<!-- pomi-wide-card -->

### Health and healing concerns

- You are pregnant or nursing.
- You take blood thinners.
- You are prone to keloids.
- You have a medical condition that affects healing.
- You have allergies or past reactions to cosmetic products.

### Treatment area concerns

- You have irritation, cuts, sunburn, or open skin near the area.
- You have active breakouts near the treatment area.
- You have a cold sore history and want lip tint.
- You have eye irritation and want eyeliner or lash enhancement.
- You have scalp irritation and want scalp micropigmentation.

<!-- pomi-warning-box -->

### Medical questions

I can answer questions about the appointment process, but I cannot give medical clearance. For medical questions, medications, infections, skin conditions, or healing concerns, speak with your healthcare provider before scheduling.

Each service has different timing and preparation needs. Ask first if you are unsure.

_Aftercare_

## Safety continues after the appointment

Aftercare protects the treated area while it heals. Fresh pigment can look darker or brighter at first, then soften as the skin heals.

<!-- pomi-wide-card -->

### Protect the healing area

- Do not pick, scratch, rub, or peel the treated area.
- Avoid makeup directly on the healing area until cleared.
- Avoid soaking, sauna, steam, swimming, and heavy sweating while healing.
- Avoid sun exposure and harsh skincare near the area.

### Know when to ask

- Ask if you are unsure about aftercare products.
- Ask if healing looks different than expected.
- Ask before using active skincare near healed pigment.
- Contact a physician for signs of infection or medical concerns.

## Permanent Makeup Safety FAQ

<!-- pomi-faq -->

#### Is permanent makeup safe?

Permanent makeup is safest when done in a clean, controlled studio with proper sanitation, single-use tools, careful setup, and clear aftercare instructions.

#### What should I look for in a permanent makeup studio?

Look for a private, clean appointment setting, disinfected surfaces, fresh disposable barriers, single-use tools, pigment containers used for one client, gloves, masks, and clear aftercare instructions.

#### Are needles and tools reused?

No. Needles, cartridges, disposable tools, and single-use supplies are used for one client and then safely discarded.

#### Why are single-use tools important?

Single-use tools help reduce cross-contamination risk because they are not shared between clients or reused after the appointment.

#### Do you open needles in front of the client?

Yes. Needles and cartridges are opened in front of the client so you know they are new for your appointment.

#### Who should ask before booking?

Ask before booking if you are pregnant, nursing, taking blood thinners, prone to keloids, have active irritation, have a cold sore history for lip tint, have eye irritation for eye services, or recently had cosmetic treatments near the area.

#### Can I book if I have irritated skin?

Do not book over active irritation, cuts, sunburn, breakouts, cold sores, open skin, or scalp irritation. Ask first so timing can be handled safely.
